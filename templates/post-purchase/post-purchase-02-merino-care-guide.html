Five easy habits, years of wear

None of this takes effort — most of it is doing nothing at all.

Wash it less — it cleans itself

Merino’s natural fibres wick moisture and resist the bacteria that cause odour, so it stays fresh far longer than cotton. Most pieces only need washing every few wears — less laundry for you, a slower-ageing garment for the long run.

Air it out, skip the wash

A night on a hanger in fresh air lets odours and light creases lift on their own. Nine times out of ten, that’s all merino needs between wears — no machine, no effort.

When you do wash: cold and gentle

Use a cold wool or delicate cycle with a mild, wool-safe detergent. Skip the fabric softener and bleach — they coat the fibre and strip away the very softness and odour resistance you bought it for.

Lay flat to dry, never tumble

Heat is the one thing merino doesn’t love. Gently reshape while damp and dry flat, away from direct heat — it keeps the fit true and the fabric soft, wash after wash.

De-pill gently, store it folded

A little pilling is normal with any natural wool. Lift it away with a fabric comb or pill remover — never pull. Between seasons, fold it (don’t hang) and keep it somewhere cool and dry; a cedar block keeps it fresh.
