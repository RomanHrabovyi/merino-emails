Why merino performs

Five things the fibre does — and what each one means for you.

It regulates your temperature

Merino’s crimped fibres trap warm air when the cold sets in and let heat escape as you warm up. One layer keeps you comfortable across a surprising range of conditions — warm when it’s cold, cool when it’s hot.

It resists odour, naturally

The fibre manages moisture and neutralises the bacteria behind smell, so it stays fresh through days of wear. That’s exactly why it needs washing so rarely — the fabric does the work for you.

It breathes and wicks moisture

Each fibre pulls moisture vapour off your skin and releases it into the air, so you stay dry and comfortable whether you’re moving or still — none of the clammy cling of synthetics.

It’s natural and renewable

Merino grows on free-roaming sheep, not in a factory. It renews season after season, contains no plastic, and biodegrades naturally at the end of a long life — performance that’s easier on the planet.

It’s built to outlast fast fashion

A merino fibre bends back on itself tens of thousands of times without breaking. Looked after simply, one piece outlasts a drawer of synthetic throwaways — the quiet opposite of fast fashion.

Where it comes from

Finer fibre, softer feel

Our merino comes from sheep bred for exceptionally fine fibre — around 17–18 microns, a fraction of the width of a human hair. That fineness is what makes it feel soft against the skin instead of scratchy like old-school wool. Ethically sourced, carefully spun, and built into pieces made to be worn for years — not thrown away after a season.
